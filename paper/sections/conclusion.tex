\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Under chronological replay, new attack classes and a small, delayed labelling budget, detectors trained once
collapse and simple retraining forgets or floods analysts with reviews. \nova{} combines class-conditional
open-world rejection with recalibration, a novelty-weighted query, conservative label propagation, a
class-balanced memory and a guarded deployment loop. It matches the strongest baseline on open-world macro-F1 on
both CIC datasets, leads it clearly when only 1\% of CIC-IDS2017 is labelled, and flags significantly fewer benign
flows on all three datasets. On UNSW-NB15 its per-class recalibration avoids the stale-threshold failure that
pulls every replay-based method below the never-updated model. Short-lived attacks that look benign, and the
transfer of its gains to neural classifiers, remain open problems.
