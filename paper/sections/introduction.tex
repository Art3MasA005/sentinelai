\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Flow-based intrusion detectors routinely report accuracies above 99\% on public benchmarks such as
CIC-IDS2017~\cite{sharafaldin2018}. Most of these results come from random train/test splits, in which flows
of the same attack session appear on both sides of the split. When the same data is split by time, reported
F1 scores fall to 0.6--0.7~\cite{nismara2025temporal}, and careful sequence evaluations find that model
architecture matters far less than the evaluation protocol~\cite{moczkodan2026transformers}. These are two
of the pitfalls catalogued by Arp et al.~\cite{arp2022dos} and Pendlebury et al.~\cite{pendlebury2019tesseract}.

A deployed detector faces three further conditions that most evaluations leave out.
\emph{New attack classes} appear after training: a model that only knows the classes it was trained on assigns
them to some known class, most often to benign traffic. \emph{Labels are scarce}: an analyst can inspect a small
fraction of the flows. \emph{Labels are late}: ground truth for today's traffic arrives after today's decisions
were made. Finally, every model update is a deployment, and a bad update degrades detection silently unless
something checks it.

Prior work addresses parts of this picture. Drift detectors and rejection
schemes~\cite{jordaney2017transcend,barbero2022transcending,yang2021cade} flag drifting samples but do not
adapt. Adaptive NIDS~\cite{andresini2021insomnia,zhang2024aoc,zhang2025ssf,mousavipour2025shift} update a
binary detector, often from pseudo-labels, and rarely model label delay. Active-learning
approaches~\cite{chen2023continuous,gupta2025netguard} choose which samples to label but assume a closed set
of classes. Open-set NIDS~\cite{zhang2020osr,corsini2023ood} reject unknown traffic on static splits.

\textbf{This paper.} We evaluate all of these concerns at once and propose \nova{} (Novelty-aware, Open-world,
Validated Adaptation). Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An evaluation protocol for drift-aware open-world NIDS: chronological replay in windows, a label oracle
        that enforces a fixed budget and delay for every method, and metrics that stay comparable when methods
        learn different classes (\cref{sec:problem}).
  \item \nova{}, which combines class-conditional $k$-NN rejection with per-class recalibration, a
        novelty-weighted and cluster-diverse query, agreement-checked label propagation, a benign-weighted
        class-balanced memory, and a deployment guard with rollback (\cref{sec:method}).
  \item Re-implementations of four published approaches under the same protocol, model and labelling cost, an
        ablation of every \nova{} component, and results on three datasets from two laboratories
        (\cref{sec:setup,sec:results}).
  \item An open-source research console (SentinelAI) that stores the configuration, data hash and per-flow
        decision of every run, so each number in this paper can be reproduced.
\end{enumerate}
