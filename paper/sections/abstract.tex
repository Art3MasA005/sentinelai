\begin{abstract}
Network intrusion detectors are trained once, then meet drifting traffic and attack classes that did not exist
at training time, while analysts can label only a small and late fraction of the flows. We study these
conditions together on chronological replays of CIC-IDS2017, CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and UNSW-NB15, with a fixed label
budget and a one-window label delay. We propose \nova{}, an adaptation loop that rejects flows far from every
known class with a class-conditional $k$-nearest-neighbour detector, recalibrates it for learned classes, spends
the label budget on novel and diverse traffic, propagates analyst labels to agreeing neighbours, keeps rare
attacks in a class-balanced memory, and deploys an update or a rollback only after a hold-out check. Against
eight methods under the same labelling cost, including re-implementations of INSOMNIA, NetGuard and
class-balanced replay, \nova{} matches the strongest baseline on open-world macro-F1 on both CIC datasets (0.768
against 0.756 and 0.722 against 0.722) while flagging significantly fewer benign flows on every dataset (e.g.\
2.8\% against 4.5\%). On UNSW-NB15, stale rejection thresholds pull every replay-based method below the
never-updated model; \nova{} stays level with it, and an ablation shows that per-class recalibration is the
reason.
\end{abstract}
