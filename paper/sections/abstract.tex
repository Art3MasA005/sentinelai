\begin{abstract}
Network intrusion detectors are trained once, then meet drifting traffic and attack classes that did not exist
at training time, while analysts can label only a small and late fraction of the flows. We study these
conditions together on chronological replays of CIC-IDS2017, CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and UNSW-NB15, with a fixed label
budget and a one-window label delay. We propose \nova{}, an adaptation loop that rejects flows far from every
known class with a class-conditional $k$-nearest-neighbour detector, spends the label budget on novel and diverse
traffic, propagates analyst labels to agreeing neighbours, keeps rare attacks in a class-balanced memory, and
deploys an update or a rollback only after a hold-out and a forgetting check. Against eight methods under the
same labelling cost, including re-implementations of INSOMNIA, NetGuard and class-balanced replay, \nova{} reaches
an open-world macro-F1 of 0.768 on CIC-IDS2017 (best baseline 0.756) at a 2.8\% false-positive rate (4.5\%).
With 1\% of the flows labelled, its lead grows to nine points. On UNSW-NB15, where stale rejection thresholds pull
every other adaptive method below the never-updated model, \nova{} is the only one above it. On CSE-CIC-IDS2018 it
ties the strongest baseline with 35\% fewer false positives.
\end{abstract}
